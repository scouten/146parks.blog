Nestled along the west shore of Hood Canal, Lilliwaup Tidelands State Park is ... shall we say ... subtly marked, but offers some nice views of the rocky shoreline.

<!-- more -->

This park starts with a rather nondescript stairway from an unmarked parking lot just north of the village of Lilliwaup.

**An important caution:** This park is entirely tidal. Every guide I read advises you to check the tide charts and time your visit carefully. Given that most of the beach shows signs of barnacles all the way up to the treeline, I echo that advice. Time your visit so you can leave comfortably ahead of a rising tide. I was there for a little more than an hour, which was enough time to hike most of the way up the park property and back while the tide was at its lowest.

The beach, once you get there, is very rocky. Some rocks come with odd graffiti messages.

The guides describe this as an "easy" hike. I understand how they get there, because there is absolutely no elevation gain other than the stairway, but ... walking along the rocks isn't exactly easy. They're constantly shifting out from under you, so it's kind of slow going.

This entire park runs immediately parallel to Highway 101. You almost never _see_ the traffic on the highway, but you do always _hear_ traffic. Not especially loudly, but it is a factor when hiking here.
