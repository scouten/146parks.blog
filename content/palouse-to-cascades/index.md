Here in Washington state, we are blessed to have several long-running rails-to-trails projects. The premiere one is the 404-km / 251-mile Palouse to Cascades State Park Trail, running from just outside Seattle to the Idaho border along the former Milwaukee Line railroad.

<!-- more -->

I had a few days this week to sample the trail and chose to explore several segments of the trail between Cedar Falls and the Columbia River.

## Cedar Falls Trailhead

My journey began at the western end of the trail, near North Bend.

## Renslow Trestle

Having driven I-90 many times under this trestle, I had always assumed that the bridge was somehow modified to accomodate the freeway that now lies beneath it.

Not so.

{{ es_cdn_image(id = "es-266-1017", cdn_version = "v1", cdn_key = "img/v1/2026/06/es-266-1017", caption = "Boylston Road, east of Kittitas, Washington", alt = "A tall steel railroad trestle labeled “Palouse to Cascades State Park Trail” spans a highway cutting through dry, sagebrush-covered hills under a heavy overcast sky. A dark semi-truck hauling a gray hopper trailer travels the near lane while a yellow box truck and several cars move along the curving road beneath the bridge.") }}

Turns out the trestle has always had these large gaps. If you get yourself up to the south end of the bridge, you get this fun view of the freeway below. And there's a fun historical photo taken circa 1964 that shows the trestle with scrub land underneath it; the freeway now conveniently fits through these gaps.

{{ es_cdn_image(id = "es-266-1027", cdn_version = "v1", cdn_key = "img/v1/2026/06/es-266-1027", caption = "Renslow Trestle, Palouse to Cascades State Park Trail, east of Kittitas, Washington", alt = "A wide view of a divided highway curving through arid, sagebrush-covered hills and rolling grassland, with several cars and semi trucks traveling the lanes and a two-lane road with guardrail branching off to the left. A vast blue sky filled with scattered clouds stretches over the landscape, with distant hills and cultivated fields on the horizon.") }}

Farther east along the trail, you're reminded of just how desolate it must have seemed for those who operated and used this route.

East of this point, the trail is much more difficult and rustic. The state requires pre-registration and more detailed preparation for those who intend to hike or bike the trail, so my exploration turned back toward some more parts of the western sections.
